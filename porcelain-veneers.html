Porcelain Veneers

A dental veneer is a thin, durable porcelain shell custom-fabricated to cover the front of a tooth. Veneers can restore the appearance of teeth with chips, cracks, and stains, close small gaps between teeth, and reshape misshapen or slightly misaligned teeth.

The veneer process

Veneers are a popular choice for cosmetic enhancement because they offer a straightforward, natural-looking way to create a brighter, more uniform smile. The process is typically completed over several appointments:

Consultation. You and your dentist discuss your goals for your smile and whether you're a good candidate for veneers.

Examination. Your teeth and gums are examined, with measurements, photographs, X-rays, and impressions taken as needed.

Preparation. A small amount of enamel is removed from the front of the tooth so the veneer will fit flush with the tooth surface. An impression is taken of the prepared tooth and the surrounding teeth, and a shade is chosen for the porcelain.

Fabrication. The veneers are lab-fabricated to the desired shape and size, using the dental impression and your dentist's detailed instructions for a precise fit and tint.

Application. Once ready, the veneers are placed at a final appointment. After checking for shape, color, and fit — and making adjustments if necessary — your dentist cleans the teeth and bonds the veneers in place with dental cement.

Are veneers right for you?

Before deciding on veneers, it's worth discussing a few things with your dentist:

Veneers are permanent. Prepping for a veneer requires removing a small amount of tooth enamel, so a veneer will need to be replaced if it's ever damaged or dislodged. Proper care keeps veneers looking their best.

Veneers aren't for everyone. Teeth and gums must be healthy before beginning the procedure, and more serious orthodontic misalignments or habits like tooth grinding and jaw clenching can make veneers impractical.

The advantages of veneers. While there are less expensive cosmetic options, veneers offer benefits that whitening and bonding alone don't:

  • Veneers cover staining that whitening treatments can't eliminate.
  • The porcelain is semi-translucent, like real enamel, so veneers look more natural than bonding and resist stains better.
  • Veneers are less vulnerable to chipping than bonding and typically last ten to fifteen years with proper care.

While the cost of veneers is comparable to crowns, veneers preserve more of the tooth's underlying structure. Talk to Dr. Doshi about whether veneers are the right way to enhance your smile.
