Oral Hygiene

Deep Cleanings

A deep cleaning — also known as scaling and root planing — is a non-surgical treatment used to treat gum disease by removing plaque and tartar from below the gumline and smoothing tooth roots so gums can reattach and heal.

When a deep cleaning is needed

A routine cleaning removes buildup above the gumline, but once plaque and tartar spread beneath it, a deeper approach is needed. Signs that a deep cleaning may be recommended include:

  • Gums that bleed easily when brushing or flossing
  • Persistent bad breath
  • Gum recession or pockets forming between teeth and gums
  • Loose or shifting teeth
  • A diagnosis of early to moderate periodontal (gum) disease

What to expect

The procedure is typically done in two parts: scaling, which removes plaque and tartar from the tooth surface and root, and root planing, which smooths the root surface so gum tissue can reattach more easily. Local anesthetic is used to keep the area comfortable during treatment.

Some mild soreness or sensitivity for a day or two afterward is normal. A follow-up visit is usually scheduled to check healing and measure how well the gums have responded to treatment.

Why it matters

Left untreated, gum disease can progress and eventually lead to bone loss and tooth loss. A deep cleaning is one of the most effective ways to stop that progression early and help gum tissue heal, and it's often paired with a more frequent cleaning schedule afterward to keep things on track.
