Teeth Whitening

Over time, stain-causing foods and beverages, smoking, and thinning enamel can dim the brightest smile. If you're feeling self-conscious about your teeth, your dentist can help you discover the whitening treatment that's best for you.

At-home whitening

Home treatments can help reduce surface stains. These methods are less expensive than in-office whitening, but because they can't offer the strength of professional whiteners, they aren't as effective and usually require several weeks of daily application.

Whitening toothpastes and rinses. These use mild abrasives and bleaching agents, most often peroxides, to remove surface stains caused by foods, beverages, and smoking. They can also help keep teeth their brightest after a professional whitening. While they can't do much to affect tooth color or deep staining, surface stains can show some improvement after several weeks.

Whitening strips. These flexible plastic strips come in two sizes to fit both upper and lower teeth, coated on one side with a thin film of peroxide-based gel. The gel side is positioned on the front of the teeth and gently pressed into place — it needs to adhere completely for even whitening, and shouldn't touch sensitive gum tissue to avoid irritation. Strips are usually recommended twice a day for 30 minutes at a time, with total treatment ranging from several days to several weeks.

Stock gel trays. These provide a mouthguard-like appliance filled with whitening gel and placed over the teeth. Trays can come pre-filled with gel or with individual gel applications in syringes. Stock trays aren't always a perfect fit, and it's important to keep the whitening gel from contacting the gums or other soft tissue. Use time varies by manufacturer.

While all these methods can brighten your smile, some circumstances make professional whitening the better option. Professional whitening agents are more powerful and can help eliminate darker stains that over-the-counter products can't remove — for deeper or darker staining, or for those who want immediate results, in-office whitening is the best choice.

In-office treatment

An office whitening treatment is generally faster, more effective, and longer-lasting because dentists use gels with a higher concentration of peroxide than those found in OTC products. Your dentist will examine you first to make sure your teeth and gums are healthy, protect your surrounding gum tissue and mouth during the procedure, and monitor treatment throughout. Office whitening usually takes 30 to 90 minutes.

Light-activated whitening. Some office whitening treatments make use of halogen, LED, or UV lighting as part of the procedure. Light doesn't whiten teeth by itself — instead, it works with the gel solution, activating the whitening molecules and speeding up the chemical reaction that eliminates stains.

Custom gel trays. Custom trays created in the dentist's office are an option for those who want to use a whitening gel at home. Professionally crafted trays fit your teeth perfectly and allow more precise application of the peroxide gel, which is also provided. Custom trays typically require about two weeks of use for maximum whitening.

Some teeth aren't good candidates for typical whitening procedures at home or in-office. If you have deeper discoloration caused by trauma or medications taken during tooth formation, or if you have dental bonding, veneers, crowns, or bridges that are darker than your natural enamel, talk to Dr. Doshi to decide which whitening treatment or cosmetic procedure is the best option to achieve your brightest smile.
