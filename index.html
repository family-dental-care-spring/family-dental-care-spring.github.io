Three periods, one era

The Mesozoic ran from about 252 to 66 million years ago. Dinosaurs appear early in the Triassic, diversify through the Jurassic, and reach their greatest variety just before the end of the Cretaceous.

Triassic

Pangaea is a single hot supercontinent. The first dinosaurs are small, fast, and bipedal — a minor group living alongside larger reptiles.

Jurassic

The supercontinent splits and the climate turns humid. Sauropods reach enormous sizes; the first birds take to the air.

Cretaceous

Flowering plants spread and continents take modern shape. Horned and duck-billed herbivores flourish under giant tyrannosaurs.

How it ended

About 66 million years ago an asteroid roughly 10 km across struck what is now the Yucatán Peninsula, leaving the Chicxulub crater. The impact threw enough rock vapour and sulfate aerosol into the atmosphere to block sunlight for years.

Photosynthesis collapsed first, then the food chains built on it. Around three quarters of species died out, including every non-avian dinosaur. Massive volcanic eruptions in the Deccan Traps had already been stressing the climate, which is why the impact fell on an ecosystem with little margin left.

One lineage survived: small, feathered, beaked theropods. Every bird alive today — from a hummingbird to an ostrich — is a dinosaur, which makes the group one of the most successful in the history of vertebrate life rather than a story that simply ended.
